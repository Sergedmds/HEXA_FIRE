03 // CORE METRICS EXPLAINED

Measuring combustion with intellectual clarity

Wildfire data is frequently misunderstood. We separate counts, energetic output, and climate deviations into distinct physical indicators.

Unit: Count

Thermal Detections

What it measures

The total tally of satellite sensor pixels flagged above ambient thermal baseline within a given window. A single high-intensity wildfire covering multiple square kilometers will produce dozens of simultaneous detections across its perimeter.

How to interpret

Best utilized for tracking spatial distribution, perimeter growth trajectories, and fire frequency across broad landscape regions rather than counting individual independent blazes.

Direct sensor pixels SPATIAL FREQUENCY
Unit: Megawatts [MW]

Fire Radiative Power

What it measures

The instantaneous rate of radiant thermal heat released by actively burning biomass, calculated directly from 4 µm middle infrared radiances using Wooster’s empirical formulation.

How to interpret

Serves as a reliable direct proxy for fuel consumption rates (kg/s) and atmospheric smoke aerosol generation. A small, violent crown fire will yield vastly higher FRP than a broad smoldering understory burn.

Combustion energy output INTENSITY & SMOKE
Unit: % Anomaly vs Baseline

Fire Activity Index

What it measures

Current thermal hotspot density mathematically normalized against the specific 10-year day-of-year seasonal distribution for that exact biome or territory.

How to interpret

Essential for contextual analysis. It immediately isolates whether elevated fire counts are part of expected dry-season agricultural rotations or represent critical climate-driven ecological crises.

04 // SCIENTIFIC RIGOR

Understanding satellite observations

Transparent satellite science requires understanding the physical boundaries of spaceborne thermal radiometers.

1

Hotspot Detections Are Not Fire Counts

A single complex wildfire spanning several miles can trigger dozens of pixel detections in one satellite overpass. Conversely, dozens of localized agricultural debris fires within a single 1km² pixel may register as just one aggregated detection. Always treat detections as indicators of spatial extent and thermal activity rather than discrete incidents.

2

Sensor Limits & Atmospheric Obscuration

Thermal infrared radiation cannot penetrate dense convective cloud cover, heavy smoke pyrocumulonimbus blankets, or thick multi-story forest canopies. Absence of detection does not guarantee absence of surface fire. When reviewing regions with persistent cloudiness, observations represent temporal snapshots between clearings.

3

Spatial Resolution Cannot Be Directly Equated

VIIRS (375m) resolves approximately ten times more pixels per square kilometer than MODIS (1km). Due to its higher sensitivity, VIIRS consistently detects smaller, cooler fires that MODIS misses. Consequently, detection numbers from these two sensors cannot be summed or directly compared without sensor-specific calibration factors.

4

Decadal Baselines Provide Real Significance

In many ecosystems (such as the African Miombo or Brazilian Cerrado), fire is a natural, cyclical ecological process. Evaluating raw detection numbers without historical context creates misleading alarms. True climate anomalies become evident only when current detections are indexed against multi-year seasonal baselines.
